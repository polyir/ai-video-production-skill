# Short-form strategy: hooks, structure, CTAs, platform rules

Confidence: 🟢 official/peer-reviewed · 🟡 repeated by expert blogs · 🔴 inference. Platform facts as of Oct 2026 — re-verify.

## Ranking signals (Instagram Reels)

- Watch time / completion / rewatch — the main signal everywhere. 🟢
- **Sends per reach** (DM shares) — strongest for reaching non-followers; likes per reach matter more for followers. 🟢
- Two-way conversation (replies within 24 h). 🟡
- Originality: original or substantially edited content gets more distribution; mostly-reposted accounts are removed from recommendations (Apr 2026). 🟢
- Hashtags are minor; keyworded caption and on-screen text do the work. Captions/alt text of professional accounts are indexed by Google. 🟢
- Only reels ≤ 3 min are recommended to non-followers. Official length bands: 3–15 s trends, 15–60 s "sweet spot", 60 s–3 min storytelling/tutorials. 🟢 Our data: 77–85 s practical explainers held ~40% of length and won.
- Max 5 hashtags per post (comment hashtags count). 🟢
- AI disclosure: keep the platform's AI label on; profiles whose main persona is AI must be labelled. Raw human content is favoured. 🟢
- Engagement-bait ("comment 1 if you agree", "tag 5 friends") is demoted; a meaningful targeted "send this to someone who…" is fine. 🟡
- Trial Reels: shown only to non-followers, no collaborators allowed — natural A/B for "one topic, two hooks". 🟢
- Political content is limited for non-followers by default → sends, collabs and followers matter more. Graphic/violent content is usually excluded from recommendations even if not removed. 🟢/🟡

## Hooks

Three simultaneous layers in 0–3 s: **visual** (frame 0 + first motion), **spoken**, **on-screen text**. Most watch the first second muted, so text + image must work alone. Optional 4th layer: ambient sound.

Rules:
- ≤ 8 words; first spoken sentence *is* the hook, starts at 0.05 s (no silence, no "let me ask you first", no setup); main promise by second 4.
- Known story → start with its unknown twist: "We all know X. But Y mattered more."
- "Count + familiar example": "Three sentences… you've definitely heard the first one: '…'".
- Never frame the viewer as guilty or threatened; replace with a practical question or a surprising number.
- No narrative/literary/reflective sentence as a hook.
- The promise must match the content — over-promising wins the click and burns satisfaction (YouTube uses post-view satisfaction surveys). 🟢
- Specific beats vague: names, numbers, concrete situations.
- Write 3 hooks from 3 different types per reel; never the same hook type or visual family on two consecutive reels.

Hook catalogue:

| # | Type | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Everyday personal question | viewer sees themselves ("Will my salary stop the day after?") |
| 2 | Myth / counter-claim | breaks a common belief ("If there's a protest tomorrow, don't take your phone.") |
| 3 | Unexpected number | specific odd number, no intro |
| 4 | Result first | give the answer, hold the "how" |
| 5 | Warning / mistake | near danger + fix (never blame the viewer) |
| 6 | In medias res / cold open | drop into a scene |
| 7 | Pattern interrupt | visual unusual in the feed (document crossed out in red marker) |
| 8 | Message / chat | chat screen or voice note; familiar and private |
| 9 | Before/after contrast | two images side by side in second 1 |
| 10 | Timed promise | "3 things, 60 seconds" |
| 11 | Send trigger | names a recipient from the start |
| 12 | Shocking documented quote | real, sourced line |

Problem: high skip + good retention = broken hook, not a broken video. 🟡

## Reel structure: problem → gap → answer → efficacy → honest limit → invite

1. 0–3 s **problem awareness**: name the problem in the viewer's words in a familiar scene.
2. By 4 s **promise + one bounded gap**: a specific question + one "but" answered in this reel. Curiosity needs a base of knowledge first (Loewenstein information gap; inverted-U — some info creates curiosity, too much satisfies it). 🟢
3. By 10 s **source** (document + page).
4. Middle: **answer early**, then mechanism. Fictional scenes are labelled ("imagined reconstruction") and never replace evidence.
5. **Efficacy**: pair every threat with a doable action and why it works (EPPM; fear appeals work on average, stronger with efficacy). 🟢
6. Close the gap by ~70–80 s with exactly what was promised; if the source has no answer, say so (trust).
7. **Invite**: one optional, relevant ask.

Audience awareness (Schwartz): unaware → familiar situation → hidden problem → consequence → one answer → evidence/limits; problem-aware → name it → confirm briefly → **answer early** → show how → why it works; solution-aware → known solution → one key distinction/limit → evidence → when it applies.

Social-movement framing (Benford & Snow): diagnostic (what's broken) → prognostic (what fixes it) → motivational (why act). 🟢
Outrage at an out-group drives shares but not trust or follows; best mix = tension in the hook, hope and solution at the end (the cold → warm arc). 🟢/🔴

## Explainers

- Result first, then problem, explanation, and a practical consequence or loop back.
- One idea per scene; voice and visual reinforce the same idea. Biggest risk: the explanation jumps ahead of the viewer.
- Segmenting (Mayer): visible chapters ("1 of 3"), chapter progress bar, numbered rules with source chips. Pre-train key terms once, briefly. 🟢
- Signaling: highlight what matters *now* with motion, colour or zoom; decoration doesn't help. 🟢
- Metaphor only when it maps one meaning directly (a narrowing pipe = bottleneck). Show instead of tell. Scale comparisons are strong for numbers.
- Full captions vs redundancy principle: keep captions (muted viewers), consider emphasising only the key word per line.
- Visual language drives retention (full-frame cinematic shots, a cut every 4–8 s); topic drives shares and reach. Improve both independently.
- Practical, sourced checklists that give the **reason** for each item are saveable; generic unsourced advice isn't.

## Endings / CTAs

- Priority 1, in every reel: **follow with a reason** — best is a named teaser of the next part ("Next: where to keep your phone if there's a protest tomorrow. Follow so you don't miss it."). Alternatives: series' training value, or the narrator as a person. Rewrite per topic, never copy. Named teasers correlated with 2–3× follower conversion in our data (n small). 🟡
- Priority 2: a question about the content (two-option or open) for comments.
- Share invite only on practical/informational posts, with a specific recipient from the topic.
- Placement: voice = follow; on-screen = content question (or share invite); caption = comment question + follow. Last ≤ 4 s.
- Audience safety: never ask anything revealing the viewer's group, members, location, city, job, role, arrest history or plans. Never call DMs or fake accounts "safe".
- Personal brand: the profile promotes the creator first; don't pin content just because it performed.

## Loop shorts (~30% of shorts ≤ 90 s, topics about cycles/repetition)

- Text: the last sentence's first half flows into the opening hook ("…and that's why, every time a dictatorship falls," → "revenge is not justice.").
- Audio: last-second ambience = first second; no fade, no end hit, cut the final breath.
- Image: last shot = first shot or its continued camera move; no end card; the hook title already visible in the last frames.
- Comment question / share invite come before the loop sentence.
- Tag them (`-loop`) in analytics to compare replay rate and watch time.

## Covers and frame 0

- Frame 0 = the cover; short, legible title; check at small size.
- Rotate cover templates and frame-0 subjects — eight identical covers in a row and four consecutive reels opening on the same type of face close-up correlated with rising skips. Decouple frame 0 from the grid cover if needed.
- Safe zone 1080×1920: keep faces/text away from the bottom ~250–350 px and the right-hand button column; preview in the app.

## Framing speakers in vertical video

1. Eye line on the upper third (~35–40% of height, ~640–770 px of 1920), subject centred horizontally.
2. Headroom shrinks as the shot tightens; in close-ups the top edge can touch the forehead.
3. Two-person edits: same eye line, headroom and head size across cuts (eye-line difference < 5%); also on two-person covers.
4. 16:9 → 9:16: frame from the eyes, not the body; if the body doesn't reach the bottom, fade the bottom into a dark band.
5. Captions in the chest/lower band, never over eyes or mouth. Grab a still per speaker and measure before rendering.

## Collabs and growth

- Collab reach is closer to a weighted average than a sum; the smaller account gains most. 🟢
- Story shares by other accounts count as views of the original reel; big-account story shares cause most spikes and are invisible to the API.
- Authority appearances on others' channels drive follows; practical "movement" reels drive reach.
- Some collaborators accept and then remove the post from their grid ("partial collab") — flag it.
- Reels → reach, carousels → conversion (blog claim). 🟡
- Series-style content (episodes that reference each other) is shared more ("you have to see all of it"). 🟡

## Content variety and production rules

- Don't repeat an episode's six shorts from the same angle; at least one ties to a current event or historical witness.
- 3–5 familiar cultural/humour moments per long episode; humour from situations, never from ethnicity, religion or gender; each moment gets its own visual.
- Reused footage ≤ 25% per episode; storyboard has a fresh/reused column; recount on the real timeline before render. Prefer freely licensed real archives (Wikimedia Commons, public archives) over symbolic AI for historical evidence; log licence + author.
- Each topic in each language is its own video — no bilingual videos.
